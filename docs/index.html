Video-to-Music Generation for Gameplay Videos

Felipe Marra Lucas N. Ferreira

Abstract

Video-to-music models are rapidly evolving, with recent approaches focusing on film and music video domains, typically adopting autoregressive encoder–decoder transformers with pre-trained components. Since multiple models have been proposed concurrently, they have not yet been systematically compared, and the question of how different visual encoders affect the quality of the decoded music remains open. In this paper, we address this gap using the retro video game domain, which offers a more structured evaluation setting and facilitates the collection of clean, paired data. We introduce a new dataset consisting of 485 hours of high-quality SNES soundtracks paired with gameplay videos, specifically curated to exclude voice-overs and sound effects. We systematically compare frozen and unfrozen encoders, including ViViT, ViT, and T5, using a MusicGen decoder. Our results demonstrate that ViViT outperforms ViT and T5, and that unfreezing encoders provides no significant performance gain. Finally, a listening test shows that our simplified ViViT-based model outperforms GVMGen and performs comparably to OSSL, despite having fewer parameters and less inductive bias.
